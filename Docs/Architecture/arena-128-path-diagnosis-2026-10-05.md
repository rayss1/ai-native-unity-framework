## Separate hypotheses

Room turnover: client-side Profile RPCs take up to2.63s, while new settlement TCP connections average3.5ms. The longest replacement spends8.043s confirming settlement and2.004s on the next room's readiness/join. Replay-visible and capacity-release queries occur after settlement and measure only residual waiting, not total write/release cost.

Each next-generation roster prepares eight accounts by registering and logging in, causing16 PBKDF2-SHA256 computations of210,000 iterations. PlayerService performs these synchronously on the Fantasy Player message context, including registration before its first database await. Multiple preparations overlap the longest settlement window. This establishes a blocking mechanism and temporal overlap; it does not yet quantify the contribution to each RPC delay or prove the database is slow. Measure KDF duration, thread/context and alignment with retained phase records before choosing an isolation fix.

Client pacing: each room shares one DriveRoom loop. Transport SendAsync normally completes synchronously after bounded enqueue, so awaiting eight sends is not eight network round trips. Independent transport Scenes do not give DriveRoom dedicated scheduling. Receive/decode work precedes the send-deadline test, and its time is excluded by the earlier timestamp. Existing missed-deadline counts cover the whole generation, not just measurement; they must not be treated as measured-window counts. Measure receive work, send preparation/enqueue, delay/resume, deadline lateness and GC/process conditions before changing pacing.
